## 19. Weaknesses

| dimension | specific_weakness | consequence | supporting_evidence | feasible_improvement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| data quality | Missingness, anomalies, and label correctness are assumed rather than audited. | Faulty observations could propagate through all five questions. | Assumption A2 and absence of an audit table. | Mechanically report missingness, duplicates, ranges, and label consistency before modeling. |
| validation | Q1 does not define the relationship among five-fold CV, train/CV/test sets, feature selection, and final voting. | Perfect scores cannot be interpreted as clean out-of-group generalization. | Q1 prose and tables 5-3/5-5/5-7. | Freeze duplicate-aware common folds and perform all feature selection inside folds. |
| statistical inference | Q3 balances frequency but not B_peak and assumes sample independence. | Factor effects may retain operating-condition confounding and standard errors may be optimistic. | Sampling description and assumptions A4/A6. | Restrict to common support or use regression/ANCOVA with overlap diagnostics and grouped uncertainty. |
| reproducibility | Q4 prose says 80/20 while appendix code uses 70/30. | Reported metrics cannot be tied unambiguously to an executable split. | Main text and appendix CatBoost code. | State one split, publish indices, and share a single evaluation script. |
| leakage | Construction of the IGSE prediction feature is not shown relative to the train/test boundary. | If fitted globally, the feature may carry test-target information and inflate improvement. | Q4 feature description; absent IGSE training code. | Fit all mechanistic parameters on each training fold, then generate out-of-fold/held-out predictions. |
| transition | Q2 selects correction form 4, Q4 invokes form 5/IGSE, and Q5 uses a basic CatBoost rather than the selected Q4 augmented model. | The advertised end-to-end modeling chain is not internally closed. | Sections 6.5, 8.3.1–8.3.3, and Q5 appendix code. | Freeze one explicit artifact/interface at each handoff and reuse it unchanged downstream. |
| optimization | Q5 scalarizes differently scaled quantities with equal weights and presents no Pareto front or weight sensitivity. | The “best” solution depends on arbitrary units and cannot demonstrate multiobjective dominance. | Objective definition and tables 9-1–9-5. | Normalize objectives, report a Pareto frontier, then choose a point with stated preferences. |
| consistency | EMI is defined as `energy/(loss+energy)` in prose but computed/reported as `loss/(loss+energy)`. | Metric direction and interpretation are reversed. | Equation in section 9.1, table values, and appendix print statement. | Correct the formula/name and recompute all EMI values consistently. |
| implementation | Q5 training uses numeric waveform/material values, but `objective_function` maps them to Chinese strings before passing them to helpers that compare against numeric values. | Both one-hot blocks become all zeros during optimization, so the supplied code does not condition loss predictions on the selected waveform or material as intended. | Q5 appendix definitions of `checkFeature3`, `checkFeatureMaterial`, and `objective_function`. | Pass numeric codes directly or make both helpers and training data use the same categorical representation; add a one-row feature-vector assertion. |
| optimization validation | Stochastic optimizers are not repeated and GA/GWO parameters/code are absent. | Superiority and robustness claims are not reproducible. | Q5 results and appendix. | Use equal evaluation budgets, fixed seed sets, convergence curves, and repeated-run distributions. |
| result consistency | The recommended Q5 LER of 36.81% is not the smallest value shown in the later 48-combination tables (36.74% appears). | The final global-best wording is not supported by the paper's own table. | Tables 9-2 and 9-4; weakness section notes local optima. | Separate per-run candidate, best tabled candidate, and verified global/Pareto claim. |

## 21. Transferable Patterns

| pattern | transferable_principle | applicable_when | limitation | page_or_section |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Shape-to-feature reasoning | Derive candidate features from visible signal geometry and spectral structure before choosing a classifier. | Labeled waveform or time-series classification with physically meaningful shapes. | Must still validate against noise, phase shifts, acquisition offsets, and group leakage. | pdf_page:15; section:5.2 |
| Mechanistic baseline extension | Diagnose a physics model's systematic residual structure, then add only interpretable correction terms and compare candidates. | A known empirical law is close but misses an observed covariate. | Requires held-out validation and coefficient/residual checks to prevent opportunistic form selection. | pdf_page:31; section:6.4 |

| Explicit downstream interfaces | Treat each question's output as a frozen input artifact for the next question. | Multi-question contests with a prediction-to-optimization chain. | This is a candidate lesson inferred from the paper's transition gaps, not a demonstrated success of this implementation. | pdf_page:60; section:8.4 |
