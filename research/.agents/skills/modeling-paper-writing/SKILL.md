# Modeling Paper Writing

Use this skill when model definitions and results already exist and the task is to turn them into a clear, defensible competition paper or revise an existing draft.

## Establish the evidence base

- Identify the problem requirements, implemented pipeline, verified results, figures, tables, and unresolved limitations available for writing.
- Do not invent experiments, metrics, parameter values, citations, or conclusions. Mark missing inputs explicitly.
- Keep historical research as prior guidance only; current-problem evidence controls the manuscript.
- Do not access prohibited corpora or broaden the source boundary merely to improve prose.

## Write the argument

- Maintain the chain `task -> data -> abstraction -> model -> validation -> result -> interpretation -> conclusion`.
- Make assumptions, symbols, objectives, constraints, inputs, outputs, and interfaces between subproblems explicit.
- Explain why a method fits the task and data; sophistication alone is not a justification.
- Present validation beside the claim it supports, including baseline, leakage controls, uncertainty, robustness, or feasibility where applicable.
- Use figures and tables only when they support a specific claim and avoid duplicate displays.
- Discuss results as `result -> comparison -> interpretation -> implication`, distinguishing association from causation.
- State limitations, empirical validity domains, and competition-time trade-offs plainly.

## Revision discipline

Preserve the author's technical meaning and project style. Make surgical edits unless a full rewrite is requested. Check notation consistency, cross-references, units, numerical consistency, and whether each conclusion answers the original question.

Do not build a phrase library from excellent papers or copy their wording. Extract rhetorical function and structure only.
